Put operating cash flow beside net income for the last eight quarters, show the accruals ratio trend, tell me whether receivables and inventory are growing faster than revenue over the same windows, and say what that means for the size of our AMZN position.
ANSWER (as the gate accepted it, brackets kept):
## Bottom line

Amazon’s earnings quality shows a **recent deterioration signal, but not yet a sustained decline**. The latest quarter combines weaker cash conversion, a mildly positive accruals ratio, and sharply faster receivables growth. ## Q02-xom-room-if-oil-falls  session=sess_a9d89924bfa9
Q: How much room does Exxon have if oil falls? Give net debt to EBITDA, EBIT interest coverage and FCF to debt for the latest four quarters against the same four a year earlier, read what the 10-K says about debt maturities and floating-rate exposure, and relate that to our XOM weight and the book's beta to USO.
ANSWER (as the gate accepted it, brackets kept):
Exxon still has substantial balance-sheet room if oil falls, although its cash cushion and free-cash-flow debt coverage weakened year over year.

- **Net debt/EBITDA:** -0.02× [f_4dbdf06e532e] for FY2025 versus -0.25× [f_7551ca3c8580] in FY2024. It remained modestly net-cash, but cash and equivalents fell to $10.68B [f_574845d0aee5] from $23.03B [f_75c9f6b9e076].
- **EBIT interest coverage:** 67.91× [f_bfde4112cdcb], improving from 48.68× [f_93b6b7cba865].
- **FCF/debt:** 254.0% [f_e7782f831f53], down materially from 619.9% [f_1777ee03550f], but still indicating annual free cash flow substantially exceeded debt.

These are annual four-quarter periods through FY2025 and FY2024, not trailing four quarters through 2026-03-31.

The maturity profile does not suggest an immediate refinancing wall. The 10-K says: “These amounts exclude that portion of long-term debt, totaling $6.2 billion, which matures within one year and is included in current liabilities. The amounts of long-term debt, excluding finance lease obligations, maturing in each of the four years after December 31, 2026, are: 2027 – $2.5 billion; 2028 – $1.7 billion; 2029 – $1.7 billion; and 2030 – $5.3 billion.” [f_7148ce7d05c9] The schedule excludes finance leases.

On floating-rate exposure, Exxon describes fixed-for-floating swaps used to hedge certain fixed-rate debt, with settlements aligned to the debt’s interest payments. It says the fair-value changes offset and that: “This program was not material to the Consolidated Financial Statements.” [f_ae1191ad990d] That argues against floating-rate exposure being a major near-term vulnerability.

For the book, XOM is 4.61% [f_a0dcbb812826], worth $496K [f_1ebd3eba01d8], on the latest portfolio run dated 2026-09-10. This makes an oil downturn meaningful but not dominant at the single-name level. Exxon’s low leverage and very high coverage provide company-level absorption capacity, though the deterioration in cash and FCF/debt means less room than a year earlier.

The book’s standalone beta to USO cannot be quoted: the latest factor fit was collinear, so the USO leg was not independently determined. The fit used 750 [f_bb5e953d3663] sessions and 750 [f_1a6750e03365] observations, with maximum VIF of 16.55× [f_4049d537a5fe] and R² of 80.7% [f_dcaea88cab09]. Therefore, I would not translate an oil-price decline into a book-level return from this run. A redesigned non-collinear exposure run would change that limitation; materially weaker free cash flow, loss of the net-cash position, or a larger XOM weight would make the downside reading less comfortable.

**Bottom line:** continued expansion is not established.
