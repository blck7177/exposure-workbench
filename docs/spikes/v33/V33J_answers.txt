## Q20-lly-margins-next-year
I would not call this a clean “yes” on the evidence the desk showed.
LLY’s net margin is high and still rising on the latest read: 37.4%, versus 26.3% at the start of the 8-quarter series. The series is choppy in between — 8.48%, 32.6%, 21.7%, 36.4%, 31.7%, 34.4%, 37.4% — so the trend is upward, but not smooth.
The filing language points to the main support: “Net income and earnings per share increased in 2025, primarily due to higher gross margin, partially offset by increased marketing, selling, and administrative expenses and research and development expenses.” It also says revenue growth was “driven primarily by increased volume, partially offset by lower realized prices,” and that those volume and price effects were “primarily driven by Mounjaro and Zepbound.” So the margin expansion story is currently tied to product mix/volume leverage and gross margin.
What keeps me from saying this will keep expanding next year is that the desk does not forecast, and the filing itself shows the offsetting forces are real: higher marketing, selling, administrative, and R&D spending. The risk section also matters because the clinical pipeline and commercialization path can change the expense load and pricing/volume mix. The desk also could not compute gross margin or operating margin here because “LLY reports no gross_profit with a period” and “LLY reports no operating_income with a period.”
So my read for the book is: margins are still expanding now, but the continuation case is conditional rather than assured. The evidence supports a positive current trend, not a forecast. The biggest thing that would change that reading is if next filings show gross margin pressure from price/portfolio mix or if operating expenses rise faster than revenue.
